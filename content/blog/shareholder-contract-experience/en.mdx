# Making Shareholder Agreements Simple, Not Simplistic

**What I Learned While Designing the Shareholder Agreement Builder**

---

ZUZU has long provided automated tools for generating stock transfer and gift agreements.
But at some point, we began hearing the same questions over and over again:

> Stock transactions don’t cover everything.

> Where do I define the roles and responsibilities between co-founders?

What users were really asking for was a **shareholder agreement**.

In startup founding or stock option execution, a shareholder agreement plays a critical role in preparing for “what if” situations.

But we saw that the barrier to entry remained high:

- Users unfamiliar with shareholder agreements didn’t know where to start.
- Others tried to write one themselves — only to give up halfway without legal support.

To solve this, we realized it wasn’t enough to just automate a document.
We had to design the **user's understanding and decision-making process** itself.

---

## It Wasn’t Just a Standalone Document

Our initial approach was simple:

- Maintain our current flows for stock transfer/gift agreements
- Add a new, standalone flow for shareholder agreements (for co-founders or employees)

But very quickly, a different pattern emerged.

**Users often needed to generate a shareholder agreement while completing a stock transaction.**

These documents were deeply intertwined. They shared overlapping clauses, and splitting them into separate flows created unnecessary friction.

We had to ask:

- **How do we seamlessly connect the two when needed?**
- **And how do we preserve a consistent experience when users create each document independently?**

---

## Rethinking the Flow

### 1. Step-by-step, not all at once

Legacy contract forms often asked users to fill in everything on a single page.
But shareholder agreements are dense and nuanced — they demand reflection.

So we broke the process into four steps using a **stepper-based interface**:

1. Enter contract date
2. Register parties involved
3. Select and configure relevant clauses
4. Preview and edit the final agreement

This reduced cognitive load and made the process feel more manageable.

<br />
### 2. Answering questions before they’re asked

At every clause-selection screen, we added an **FAQ sidebar**.

Users often hesitate with questions like:

> “Do I really need this clause?”

> “Is this relevant to our company?”

So we provided explanations: <br/>
What the clause means, why it matters, and when it’s typically used — all in plain language.

Important clauses were pre-selected with “Yes” by default, while saying “No” triggered a **warning panel** that explained potential risks.

Once users completed the form, they were taken to a **live preview** of the full contract — with the ability to edit any clause directly.

## What We Were Really Solving

This project started as a document automation problem. <br/>
But we quickly realized the real challenge wasn’t the document — it was the **people** writing it.

- Users aren’t familiar with legal templates.
- They struggle to judge what’s necessary.
- They need to feel confident in what they’ve produced.

So we designed the experience around these realities.<br/>
**One decision at a time. One explanation at a time.**

Not overloading the interface with legal jargon,
but surfacing guidance at just the right moment — when it’s actually needed.

---

## Closing Thoughts — <br/>Building for Agreement, Not Just Documents

This feature wasn’t about automating paperwork. <br/>
It was about building a tool that helps people document their **mutual understanding and expectations**.

What we aimed to provide was:

- Structured inputs, not blank forms
- Decision-driven flows, not blind automation
- Editable outcomes, not static templates

Legal UX means navigating complexity with care.
It’s about breaking down ambiguity into clear decisions — without assuming the user is a legal expert.

The result wasn’t perfect. But we’re proud of how we tackled a difficult domain, and made meaningful progress toward a better contract-writing experience.

Because in the end, it’s not just about getting the document done.
It’s about making sure people understand what they’ve agreed to — and feel confident moving forward.
