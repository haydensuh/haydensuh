# Making Shareholder Agreements Accessible for Everyone

**What I learned while designing ZUZU’s shareholder agreement feature**

---

ZUZU has long offered automated document creation for stock transfer and gift contracts.
But over time, we began receiving a new pattern of questions:

> I was told to include profit-sharing rules through a shareholder agreement when transferring shares. Does ZUZU support that?

> After our stock transfer, we need to prepare a shareholder agreement. Is there a template we can use?

> After our stock transfer, we need to prepare a shareholder agreement. Is there a template we can use?

These questions pointed to a growing need: **shareholder agreements**.

Shareholder agreements are essential—especially in cases like co-founding a company or exercising stock options—because they help clarify expectations and protect all parties involved.

Yet for many users, they remain a daunting task:

- Users unfamiliar with shareholder agreements don’t know where to begin.
- Many abandon the process when attempting to draft them without legal help.

To address this challenge, we needed more than document automation. <br/>
We needed to rethink how users **understand, navigate, and make decisions** throughout the agreement-creation process.

---

## When a single flow couldn’t describe the real user journey

At first, we planned to introduce the feature through two separate flows:

- The **existing stock transfer/gift contract flow**
- A new standalone flow for **co-founder or employee shareholder agreements**

But soon, an unexpected scenario surfaced:

**Users often need to draft an employee shareholder agreement while completing a stock transfer.**

The two contracts overlap in content, and some clauses are duplicated across both. <br/>
This meant that simply adding a separate menu wasn’t enough—we wouldn’t be capturing the actual user experience.

So we reframed the problem:

- **How should the experience work when both agreements are completed together?**
- **And how do we maintain consistency when they’re completed separately?**

This became the starting point for redesigning the flow.

---

## Starting with document-level analysis

To understand how the flows should connect and how to maintain a consistent experience,
we first conducted a structural analysis of real shareholder agreements.

We compared co-founder agreements with employee shareholder agreements, identifying:

- Clauses common to both

- Clauses required only for specific scenarios

- Clauses that overlap with stock transfer/gift contracts

## UX strategies to reduce decision-making burden

### 1. Stepper-based structure

The previous contracts were created on a single-page input form. <br/>
But shareholder agreements require deeper consideration for each clause.

To reduce cognitive load, we moved to a **stepper-based multi-step process**:

1. General contract information
2. Party information
3. Clause selection and condition setup
4. Preview and edit

Breaking the flow into steps helped users avoid overload and understand decisions one at a time.

<br />
### 2. FAQ section for contextual guidance

Users frequently hesitate at the clause-selection stage:

> Do I really need this clause?

> Is this relevant to our situation?

To support decision-making, we introduced a **left-hand FAQ section** on each clause page.

Each section explained:

- Why the clause matters
- When it is typically needed
- How most users decide

For high-importance clauses, the default was set to “Yes.”
If the user selected “No,” a warning panel highlighted potential risks and consequences to ensure informed decisions.

## Designing for user decision-making—not automation

The project began as a simple extension of our contract automation system. <br/>
But it quickly became clear that the real challenge wasn’t generating the document,
but enabling users to **understand, choose, and verify** the content.

Our research surfaced three core needs:

- Users are not familiar with legal terminology or contract structure.
- They struggle to assess which clauses are relevant.
- They need control over the final document and wording.

Thus, the real task was to create a **UX structure that supports complex decision-making**, not just automate text generation.

We designed a contextual guidance model that avoids overwhelming explanations,
but provides precise support at exactly the moments users need to make a decision.

---

## Final thoughts

This project was not about generating a legal document. <br/>
It was about building a **user-centered process** for drafting shareholder agreements.

It required much more than screen design—it involved:

- Clause-level structural analysis
- Mapping user decision points
- Crafting an information-exposure strategy tailored to the legal domain

The outcome:

- Users unfamiliar with contracts can understand why certain clauses matter
- Experienced users can adjust the language and reflect actual agreements
- Both groups can complete a legally meaningful document with confidence

The true value of this work wasn’t automation. <br/>
It was creating a foundation where users can **make informed decisions, review their choices, and confidently finalize their agreements.**
